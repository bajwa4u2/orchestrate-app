How Orchestrate works

One business. One relationship. Market opportunity to payment.

Kestrel Mechanical Services is a 22-person commercial mechanical contractor. Below is its commercial life running on Orchestrate, followed all the way through, once.

Demonstration scenario

Kestrel and the counterparty in this walkthrough are fictional. Roughly six to eight minutes to read; the section headers alone take about ninety seconds.

Scene 1 · Business intent

It starts with what the business is trying to achieve.

Not with a contact list. Orchestrate needs to know what Kestrel sells, where it works and who it serves, because everything downstream is judged against that.

Business · Commercial intent
Kestrel Mechanical Services
Commercial mechanical contractor · 22 people · Southeast Michigan
What we offer
Commercial HVAC installation, planned maintenance and emergency service
Who we serve
Property managers, facilities operators and general contractors with multi-site portfolios
Where we operate
Wayne, Oakland and Macomb counties
How work arrives
Proposal and tender, delivered in stages, invoiced against accepted work

Why this matters: a system that begins at "add a contact" can only ever record what someone already decided. Beginning at commercial intent is what lets Orchestrate reason about the market at all.

Scene 2 · Market

A candidate, and the reasoning behind it.

Market is possibility, before continuity exists. A candidate is a counterparty Kestrel is evaluating, and it has to be able to answer why.

Market · Candidate
Halloway Property Group
Property management · multi-site commercial portfolio
AI reasoning
Why discovered Portfolio expansion in Kestrel's operating counties.
Why it matters Multi-site portfolios buy planned maintenance as a program rather than per call, which is the shape of work Kestrel delivers best.
Supports Three properties added within the operating area in the last two quarters.
Argues against An incumbent service provider is likely already in place. No signal either way.
Decided Worth pursuing, pending a human decision.

What AI is doing here: assembling the evidence and stating the commercial reason. What it is not doing: deciding. The candidate is presented with what argues against it, because a judgment you cannot argue with is not a judgment.

Scene 3 · The decision to pursue

A relationship begins when a person decides it should.

Not when a score crosses a threshold. Kestrel's owner reviews the candidate and chooses to pursue. That admitted decision is what establishes durable commercial context.

Today · Waiting on you
Judgment required
One candidate is ready for a decision
Human Halloway Property Group. Pursue, or set aside with a reason.
Decided Pursue. A relationship is established, and the market judgment that led here is kept as its origin.

Not every counterparty becomes a relationship. That distinction is the difference between a system that holds a company's real commercial world and one that holds a list.

Scene 4 · Authority

The moment the governance is real.

Kestrel prepares to act. Orchestrate checks who is allowed to cause this, every time, at the point of action, in plain words.

Relationship · Halloway Property Group
Allowed Introduce Kestrel to Halloway. This is a communication act, and the operations lead holds communication authority. It proceeds.
Stopped Propose service terms. This creates a contractual commitment. The operations lead holds communication authority, which does not cover it.
What is missing Contractual authority for this person, granted by the business.
How to resolve The owner grants it in Business → people and authority, or takes the act himself.
Resolved The owner grants contractual authority, scoped to this area of the business. The act proceeds, and the record says who granted it, and when.

Communication authority is not contractual authority is not financial authority. Being able to do something is not the same as being able to delegate it. One refusal, resolved in a sentence, and the work continues. That is what governed execution looks like in practice.

Scene 5 · Communication

Drafted by AI, sent under Kestrel's own identity, honest about what happened next.

Grounded in Kestrel's declared offer and operating area. Nothing is asserted about Halloway that Orchestrate does not hold as an admitted fact.
Sent under Kestrel's own verified business identity, by an authorized person.
Accepted Accepted by the destination mail server. That is acceptance for delivery, which is not the same as delivered, and neither is the same as read.

Sent is not delivered, and delivered is not read. The system records the evidence it actually has, and where it has none it says so rather than showing a green tick. An operator who has waited on a tender knows the difference between "they said no" and "it never arrived."

Scene 6 · Engagement, agreement and obligations

A conversation becomes terms. Terms become work that is owed.

An engagement is one undertaking inside the relationship. Agreement, obligations, execution, acceptance, invoice and payment are stages of that one undertaking, presented as one experience and kept as distinct commercial facts.

Obligations are real objects with owners and dates, not line items on a document. It is what allows an invoice, later, to cite the work it is for.

Scene 7 · Execution and acceptance

The step most software skips.

Work is performed against the obligations. Then Halloway accepts it. That is an admitted act by an identified person, not a status somebody toggled.

Engagement · Execution
Site A
Service completed · evidence attached
Site B
Service completed · evidence attached
Site C
Service completed · evidence attached
Accepted The facilities director at Halloway accepts delivery of all three visits.
Recorded Who accepted, what they accepted, and when.

An invoice that is not anchored to accepted work is a dispute waiting to happen. Acceptance is represented as its own commercial event because, in many of the workflows Orchestrate initially targets, accepted work determines when the next billing step becomes accountable. Deposits, retainers and staged billing are ordinary commercial structures, and the record has to hold each of them honestly.

Scene 8 · Invoice and payment

The invoice cites the work it is for.

Engagement · Invoice
Issued to
Halloway Property Group
For
Q1 planned maintenance, three obligations, all accepted
Anchored to
The acceptance recorded in scene 7, by name and date
Authority
Financial, a distinct authority from communication or contractual
Paid Payment received and reconciled against the invoice.

Agreement, obligation, acceptance, invoice and payment are five different commercial facts. Fragmented workflows often flatten or separate them, which makes the causal commercial record hard to reconstruct later. Keeping them distinct is what makes the record hold up months afterward, when somebody asks what happened.

One counterparty. One continuous record of what was judged, agreed, delivered, accepted and paid, and under whose authority each of those happened. That record is the asset. The next engagement begins with it, instead of beginning again.

That is the whole product: a business relationship carried from market opportunity to payment, without losing who authorized what along the way.
