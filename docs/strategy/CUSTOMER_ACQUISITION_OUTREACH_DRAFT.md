# Orchestrate outreach and discovery call: DRAFT for founder approval

Status: DRAFT. Nothing here is sent until the founder approves the wording. Messages go from the
founder's own mailbox, one at a time, personalised with one real detail about the firm.

Rules: no claim of customers, pilots or results; no invented urgency; one link; a plain way to say
no. US commercial email law (CAN-SPAM) needs a real sender, a truthful subject, the business postal
address and an opt-out line.

---

## 1. First email

**Subject:** Quote to payment at {Firm}

Hi {First name},

I saw {one real detail: a recent project, a public bid award, a line from their site}.

I ran a contracting and municipal works business in Oman for nineteen years: tenders, crews,
staged delivery, sign-offs and chasing payment. Most of what went wrong happened between tools:
what was promised in the proposal was not what the crew saw, and what was signed off was not what
got invoiced.

I now build software for exactly that line, from the first opportunity through the agreement,
delivery, sign-off, invoice and payment, kept in one record. Nothing goes out in your company's name
without a person approving it. It is called Orchestrate, and it is new: I am looking for a few
Michigan firms to try it on a real job and tell me honestly where it falls short.

Would you give me 20 minutes to hear how {Firm} handles that today? I am in Taylor, so I can also
come to you.

Muhammad Sakhawat
Founder, Aura Platform LLC (Orchestrate)
https://orchestrateops.com/demo

Aura Platform LLC, 25426 Goddard Rd, Taylor, MI 48180
If this is not relevant, reply "no" and I will not write again.

**Word count target:** under 170 words. Personal detail line is mandatory; if there is no real
detail, the firm is not ready to contact.

## 2. One follow-up (7 to 10 days later, only if no reply)

**Subject:** Re: Quote to payment at {Firm}

Hi {First name}, one short follow-up. If a call is not useful, a one-line answer would still help
me: where does work most often slip for you, between the quote, the job, sign-off or getting paid?

Muhammad

(Same address and opt-out lines.) After this, no further messages unless they reply.

## 3. Phone / walk-in version (for firms with no email route)

"Hi, this is Muhammad Sakhawat, I am in Taylor. I ran a contracting business for nineteen years and
now build software for the quote-to-payment side of it. I am not selling today. I am asking a few
owners for 20 minutes to hear how they handle it. Is {owner} the right person, and when is a good
time?"

---

## 4. Discovery call guide (20 minutes)

Goal: learn, not pitch. Show the demo only if they describe the problem themselves.

1. **The work.** What does a typical job look like, from how you find it to getting paid?
2. **Winning it.** How do bids or proposals get made, and who approves the price and terms?
3. **Handoff.** When a job is won, how does the crew or project lead learn what was promised?
4. **Changes.** How are change orders agreed and recorded?
5. **Sign-off.** Who signs off completed work, and how do you prove it later?
6. **Invoicing.** How long from sign-off to invoice to payment? What delays it?
7. **Tools.** What do you use today (email, spreadsheets, QuickBooks, Jobber, Procore, other)?
8. **The last mistake.** Tell me about the last time something was promised, sent or billed wrong.
   What did it cost?
9. **Who decides.** If a tool fixed that, who would decide to use it, and what would it need to be
   worth?
10. **Next step.** Would you try it on one real job with me helping?

Record after each call (in CUSTOMER_ACQUISITION.md pipeline): date, who, current tools, the problem
in their words, cost of the last mistake if stated, interest level, agreed next step. Quote them;
do not upgrade interest into commitment.
